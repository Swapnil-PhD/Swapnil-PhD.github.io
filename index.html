Working Papers
Credit Market, Uncertainty, and Business Cycle

Applies Bayesian Vector Autoregression to labor market dynamics, showing that omitting credit spreads and macro uncertainty misses key cyclical features — these shocks explain 38% and 32% of short-run volatility, respectively. Credit spread shocks generate boom-bust cycles while uncertainty shocks produce smoother recoveries; a structural DGE model with financial frictions reproduces and explains both patterns.

Monetary Policy Surprise and Forward Information

Shows that roughly 10% of high-frequency policy shocks can be predicted from forward information already held by the Fed and forecasters, challenging the assumption that these shocks are fully unexpected. Constructs a new shock series orthogonal to forward information and finds materially larger, more statistically significant real effects than earlier high-frequency studies report.

Non-Banking Loans and Monetary Policy Transmission in India
with Shashwat Agarwal

Uses loan-level data on banks and non-banks in India to show that contractionary monetary policy reduces bank lending but increases non-bank lending, offsetting the intended policy effect by roughly 30%.
