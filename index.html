About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in economics at Boston College, specializing in econometrics and quantitative macroeconomics. I am on the job market in 2026–2027.

My research looks at how credit markets, macroeconomic uncertainty, and monetary policy move the business cycle. I use Bayesian time-series methods and structural models with financial frictions. In my job market paper, I find that credit spread shocks and uncertainty shocks explain 38% and 32% of short-run volatility. In other work, I show that about 10% of high-frequency monetary policy surprises can be predicted from information the Fed and forecasters already had.

At Boston College, I teach undergraduate macroeconomic theory as a Teaching Fellow. I also work as a research statistician for Research Services, where I turn scanned records and credit card statements into datasets researchers can use. Before the Ph.D., I was an Assistant Manager in R&D at Hero MotoCorp and an intern in the Foreign Exchange Department of the Reserve Bank of India.
