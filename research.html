My research uses Bayesian time-series and structural methods to study credit markets, macro uncertainty, and monetary policy transmission.

Working Papers
Credit Market, Uncertainty, and Business Cycle [JMP][PDF]

Hours worked has a pronounced spectral peak at business-cycle frequencies, yet the shock that explains most of its variation shows no clear boom-bust response. I show that this shock combines two distinct forces: macro uncertainty produces a broad contraction followed by recovery, while a credit sentiment shock generates an expansion that later reverses. I develop and estimate a model in which firms borrow against collateral to finance labor costs. Uncertainty tightens financing conditions, whereas favorable productivity news that fails to materialize generates the credit boom and subsequent bust.

Monetary Policy Surprise and Forward Information

Shows that roughly 10% of high-frequency policy shocks can be predicted from forward information already held by the Fed and forecasters, challenging the assumption that these shocks are fully unexpected. Constructs a new shock series orthogonal to forward information and finds materially larger, more statistically significant real effects than earlier high-frequency studies report.
